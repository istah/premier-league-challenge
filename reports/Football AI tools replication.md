# Replicate the human-in-the-loop pattern, not the vendors' models

Real football clubs use AI mainly for pre-match opponent work, set-piece planning and daily load flags. No club has publicly documented a validated live AI that recommends substitutions or formations, and no club has publicly documented a multi-agent LLM staff. That gap suits a 2-person team. The tools that are honestly replicable on synthetic, stats-only data are the patterns, not the models: evidence-linked summaries (Hudl style), a short flag list with contributing factors (Zone7 style), physics-based pitch control and xT (Liverpool and published methods), and retrieval of similar set-pieces (TacticAI style). Each of these can be built in days. Their accuracy cannot be shown, because on synthetic data any "result" measures our generator. The strongest and most defensible pitch is therefore "explainable, auditable decision support where the coach decides", paired with a synthetic dataset that is visibly consistent and honestly labelled. The week's best investment is data consistency checks and a claim-to-data fact-checker with an automated eval, because the public record shows nobody has published such an eval. Four items need primary-source checks before the demo, and one of them (registration status) may already be due today.

## Real clubs use AI for dossiers, set pieces and flags, not live decisions

The documented clubs cluster at the same three tasks. Liverpool's lead data scientist built pitch-control models used against opponents before matches and for the team's own review afterwards, with tablets on the bench ([Training Ground Guru](https://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/william-spearman-how-liverpool-create-pitch-control)). Manchester City's SAP touchline app was used for game plans before, adjustments at half-time and review afterwards, but that is a **2018 vendor-reported** case with only player quotes as evidence ([Inside World Football](https://insideworldfootball.com/?p=109514)). Brentford's analysts use a Genius Sports platform for opposition reports and set-piece review, again a dated and secondary source ([Training Ground Guru](https://trainingground.guru/how-clubs-like-brentford-turn-data-into-an-advantage/)). Public detail on Arsenal, Brighton, Real Madrid and Bayern turned up nothing usable.

Live use is the weak spot. Liverpool's Spearman said live tracking carries roughly a **7-minute lag** and accuracy issues compared with reprocessed data, and that only a few leading Premier League teams use pitch-control-type models in practice (interview date unclear, possibly dated) ([Training Ground Guru](https://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/william-spearman-how-liverpool-create-pitch-control)). The usual matchday workflow is two analysts coding from the stand and relaying to the dugout. Our simulated tick data is therefore more "live" than anything a club actually has, and the pitch should say that. Absence of public evidence is not proof that clubs have nothing private, but we cannot claim they do. For substitution support, the only items found are academic prototypes: a rule-based fuzzy-logic system ([Wharton Sports Analytics Journal](https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FARIAS_AI_Assisted_Substitution_Decisions__A_Fuzzy_Logic1_Approach_to_Real_Time_Game_Management-1.pdf)) and a UWA paper that infers opponent tactics and recommends substitutes from live ratings ([UWA](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/temporal-match-analysis-and-recommending-substitutions-in-live-so/)). Neither was validated in live matches.

### TacticAI is the best-evidenced tool and its evidence is narrow

DeepMind and Liverpool's TacticAI predicts corner-kick receivers and shots, retrieves similar past corners and suggests adjusted player positions, trained on **7,176 corners** ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/tacticai-ai-assistant-for-football-tactics/), [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/can-ai-improve-football-teams-success-from-corner-kicks-liverpool-and-others-are-betting-it-can-225894)). Liverpool experts preferred its suggested setups about **90% of the time** in a blind test, and judged its retrieved examples relevant 63% of the time against a 33% baseline ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/tacticai-ai-assistant-for-football-tactics/)). That is expert preference, not an outcome improvement, and press coverage noted that no players had tried the suggestions on the pitch ([Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/google-deepmind-wants-to-make-ai-a-part-of-soccer/)). The June 2026 claim that Palmeiras uses an open-play extension in real time rests on one secondary, partly promotional source with no metrics ([ecosistemastartup.com](https://ecosistemastartup.com/?p=88314)); it is **unverified** and stays out of the pitch unless DeepMind's own channel confirms it. We could not read the Nature Communications paper, so its accuracy figures are also unconfirmed.

### LLM deployments are fan-facing or analyst-assist, and evidence they help is absent

The Premier League Companion is a Copilot assistant on the league's app, announced as part of a five-year Microsoft partnership; Foundry for live-match overlays was "planned" at announcement, and whether it shipped is unconfirmed ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/07/01/premier-league-and-microsoft-announce-five-year-strategic-partnership-to-personalize-the-fan-experience-with-ai-for-1-8-billion-people/)). Hudl StatsBomb's AI Player Summaries are the closest analyst-facing pattern: plain-language profiles where each line maps to underlying metrics and video, and unsupported claims are left out ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/blog/hudl-statsbomb-ai-player-summaries)). That grounding claim is the vendor's own and untested. Outside football, Oracle Red Bull Racing's strategy agent is the nearest decision-support analogue, described as working alongside human engineers, though the evidence is vendor announcement plus press ([Oracle](https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/oracle-red-bull-racing-extends-title-partnership-with-oracle-2026-02-26/)). No multi-agent architecture was publicly documented in any deployment found. One caution on trust: a Big Sky college coach said chatbot recommendations were "absurd" to someone who knows football, a single anecdote ([Spokesman-Review](https://spokesman.com/stories/2026/aug/08/big-sky-coaches-players-move-forward-cautiously-in)), while a qualitative study of analysts says they must keep proving insights are credible and fit the coach's beliefs ([arXiv 2504.16572](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16572)). Both support showing evidence per claim.

## Load and injury tools: copy the output shape, never the accuracy

Zone7 is the only vendor with a clearly described coach-facing AI output: a daily emailed list of flagged players, typically 1-2 per day at Rangers, for staff to investigate, with no published scores or thresholds ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/football/story/_/id/37613690/algorithm-really-predict-injuries)). Its headline results are club-reported before/after figures, such as Getafe's 40% then 66% fall in injury volume, with no control group, and ESPN reported no false-positive rate or sensitivity ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/football/story/_/id/37613690/algorithm-really-predict-injuries)). We found no independent evaluation. Catapult's "over 1,200 football teams" is a company claim ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/catapult-sports)). Everything vendor-side here is **vendor PR or club-reported**, not independent evidence.

The independent literature is sceptical. ACWR was endorsed by an IOC consensus in 2016 but is contested; Impellizzeri and colleagues argue it should be dismissed, and the 0.8-1.3 "sweet spot" is not a validated safe zone ([Zouhal et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138569/)). A 2025 scoping review of machine-learning injury prediction (38 studies, football most studied) found small datasets, heterogeneous injury definitions and AUC as the main metric, with tree-based models best in 60% of studies and logistic regression beating ML in 4 of 12 comparisons; its authors question clinical relevance even where AUC exceeded 0.9 ([Leckey et al.](https://www.repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/100173?show=full)). On wellness versus HRV, one small football study (n=9) found a Hooper-style wellness index more sensitive than HRV ([Rabbani 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6390199/)), and a 2016 review found subjective measures tracked load with superior sensitivity ([Saw et al.](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/50/5/281)). Neither is strong enough to say wellness beats HRV in general, but both justify weighting the check-in at least equally.

For in-match monitoring, the research is retrospective. Second-half declines in peak running demands depend on position and metric ([Biology of Sport 2023](https://www.termedia.pl/Positional-and-temporal-differences-in-peak-match-running-r-ndemands-of-elite-football,78,46979,0,1.html)), and tactical context confounds them. No source gave a numeric sprint or high-speed threshold that triggers a substitution, so any threshold in our app is a labelled demo parameter. Rules on live wearable data reaching the bench changed with the 2018/19 Laws, and we could not confirm the current wording ([IFAB circular summary](https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/surreyfa/files/referees/laws_of_the_game_2018_19_law_changes_explained_en.ashx?la=en)).

On data protection, the safe pitch wording is that real deployments would treat this as special-category health data needing a lawful basis, transparency and player access, with consent only one possible basis and questionable in employment ([Morgan Lewis, July 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/07/soccers-big-data-dilemma-who-owns-the-data-behind-the-beautiful-game)). That is gentler than the project rule's "real use needs player consent"; align the wording.

## Tactical methods are cheap to rebuild but weak at explaining results

Pitch control is the best value method. Spearman's model estimates, from player positions, velocities and arrival times, the probability each team controls the ball at each location ([Training Ground Guru](https://archive.trainingground.guru/articles/william-spearman-how-liverpool-create-pitch-control)). Open implementations exist ([Metrica pitch control](https://github.com/anenglishgoat/Metrica-pitch-control)). Expected threat (xT) is a grid model implemented in socceraction on a 16x12 grid ([socceraction docs](https://socceraction.readthedocs.io/en/stable/documentation/valuing_actions/xT.html)). Our judgement, not a sourced claim: both run on the tick engine's positions and events with a few dozen lines each. Two honesty limits apply. A study of six Premier League seasons found pitch control correlated only weakly with xG difference ([IFISC](https://ifisc.uib-csic.es/es/publications/measuring-the-pitch-control-of-professional-footba)), and Spearman himself said that without further models it is little more than a visual tool. And xT values fitted on synthetic events only reflect the generator, so ship a fixed published-style grid labelled illustrative. Use pitch control to trigger and explain ("shape changed, control shifted"), never to claim it predicts goals.

Vendor feature claims (Second Spectrum, SkillCorner, Hudl StatsBomb OBV) are marketing pages, and no independent tactical-impact study was found for them. Opta and Wyscout tactical products, PFF and Sportlogiq were not researched in depth. Treat their ideas as inspiration for event-derived stats (xG, passing networks, PPDA), not as benchmarks.

## Making the synthetic data realistic and consistent

Consistency is cheaper to win than realism, and judges can see it. Add invariants as asserts in the generator, from design practice rather than literature: goal events must equal the match score for each team; tracking minutes must match lineup and substitutions, with none after a red card or substitution; sprint distance must not exceed high-speed distance, which must not exceed total distance, in every bin; no speed above human maximum; injured players have no match minutes; wearable rows exist for each active player-day; and training heart-rate aggregates agree with session load. Use one seeded random generator per table and entity derived from a master seed, so adding a table does not shift the others, and add a CI test that the same seed gives identical file hashes. This extends the project's existing seed rule.

For realism, layer simple models, all stdlib or NumPy. Draw the scoreline from a Dixon-Coles style Poisson model with a low-score correction ([Lancaster eprint](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/19492), [penaltyblog docs](https://penaltyblog.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models/overview.html)), then allocate shots and shot xG so events always sum to the score. Generate possession as a Markov chain over pitch zones. Model fatigue with a two-component Banister impulse-response curve, but note the time constants in the notes are **unverified**. Drive HRV, output and injury hazard from a hidden "readiness" factor.

Verified calibration anchors are few. Injury incidence in European professional football was **8.0 per 1,000 hours overall, 27.5 in matches and 4.1 in training**, about a 6-7x match-to-training ratio ([Ekstrand et al. 2011](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/45/7/553)), so check generated rates fall near these. For HRV, lnRMSSD typical error is about 12% and the smallest worthwhile change about 3% ([Buchheit 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3936188/)). Position effects are directional only: wingers cover the most high-speed and sprint distance, centre-backs the least in one Premier League tracking study ([Morgans et al.](https://repositorio.ipsantarem.pt/entities/publication/8bcaff81-dc71-4ed9-9ac3-824334021b17)), and midfielders cover the most total distance ([Di Salvo et al. 2007](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-2006-924294)). The notes list typical values for xG per shot, shots per match, pass completion, running distances and PPDA, but these are **unverified background figures**. Do not use them as sourced numbers; read the papers named in the notes (Pappalardo 2019, Di Salvo 2007, Bush 2015, Rampinini 2007) and record each value in a provenance file with source, licence, date and derivation.

Licensing narrows what may touch the repo. The Wyscout public dataset is CC BY 4.0, so locally computed aggregates may be committed with citation ([figshare](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/7770599)). StatsBomb's open-data agreement bars distributing the data and commercially exploiting "any analysis derived from" it, and requires the provider's logo on published analysis ([LICENSE.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/statsbomb/open-data/master/LICENSE.pdf)); that conflicts with the no-third-party-trademark rule, so calibrate to peer-reviewed numbers instead. Metrica and SkillCorner licence terms are unclear. football-data.co.uk explicitly forbids AI training use ([football-data.co.uk](https://www.football-data.co.uk/data.php)); avoid it. Google Research Football can simulate tracking data ([arXiv 2503.19809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19809)) but our own tick engine already does the job.

Last, the circularity trap. If injuries are generated from HRV and load and a model then "finds" HRV and load predictive, the result proves nothing. Keep the latent state hidden, add label noise, let planted effects explain only a modest share of variance, include a few null decoy relationships, split by time, and keep a `ground_truth.json` outside participant-facing data. Put a banner on the README and every dashboard: "synthetic data, fictional players, calibrated to published aggregates; not evidence about real players or clubs."

## Claims it would be dishonest to make

Evidence quality sets the limits of the pitch. Say none of the following: that Zone7-style flags "predict injuries" or match any vendor (vendor and club-reported evidence only, no independent validation); that ACWR zones are validated; that any model's AUC or accuracy on our data says anything about real football; that a decline in late-game output equals fatigue (pacing and game state confound it); that clubs already run live AI substitution advice (nothing documented); that Palmeiras uses TacticAI in real time (single secondary source, unverified); that TacticAI improves results (90% is expert preference); that pitch control or xT predicts winning; that Decision-1 benchmark claims hold (self-reported by Microsoft, per [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4234290/microsoft-separates-decision-making-from-generative-ai-with-a-new-model.html)); or that our Foundry evaluators are all stable (Task Completion, Task Adherence and Intent Resolution are labelled preview in the Learn docs, per the notes' reading of [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/concepts/evaluation-evaluators/agent-evaluators)). Also avoid "Activator is GA" until checked in the portal. Safe framing: stats and API-style feeds only, simulated at a faster cadence than real live feeds, AI informs and the coach decides.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which club tool can we copy" to "which public pattern can we copy honestly." Clubs publicly document dossiers, set pieces and flag lists, the Microsoft partnership documents Foundry, agents and near-real-time insight, and nobody publishes an eval for LLM football analysis. That leaves an opening where a small team can credibly lead: an auditable multi-agent system whose every claim links to data, scored by its own eval, running on a dataset that proves its own consistency.

The practical consequence is that the two-person split should weight data quality and the fact-checker eval as heavily as agent features. Askhat's experiments become more valuable as tools the agents call, with his work on invariants and ground truth protecting the whole demo from the circularity trap. The risk to manage is overclaiming. Much of the supporting evidence is vendor PR, and several useful numbers remain unverified, so the pitch should state its limits plainly, which judges briefed on "explainable match intelligence" are likely to reward.
