# Prior art and what we adopt

Search-based, not exhaustive. Vendor blogs are weak sources; thresholds quoted below are illustrative, not industry standards.

## Commercial landscape
Hudl Sportscode (video coding), Wyscout (scouting, event data), Catapult and Kinexon (wearable load and real-time tracking), Second Spectrum and SkillCorner (tracking, broadcast / computer vision). These describe and measure. A search found **no product that recommends substitutions or in-match tactical changes**, and no club-published definition of "high block". Our positioning: decision support on top of the data, explained, with the coach in charge. (Absence from a search is not proof; say "we found none" in the pitch, never "none exist".)
Sources: [tool roundup](https://www.sportsfirst.net/post/top-football-analytics-software-companies-for-clubs-and-coaches-in-2026), [video analysis tools](https://english-programs.sportsdatacampus.com/football-video-analysis-software/)

## Play-style metrics
PPDA (opponent passes per defensive action; lower = harder press) is the standard pressing metric, with press height and success rate as complements. A rule-based classifier using PPDA bands plus pressure height is described in the [Soccer Analytics Textbook](https://socceranalytics.net/chapters/pressing-analytics.html); also [Sportmonks](https://www.sportmonks.com/blogs/pressing-intensity-football/) and a [systematic review of AI tactical analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12163489/).
**Adopt:** keep `style.py` rule-based and explainable, align bands with PPDA conventions (<=8 gegenpress, <10 high press, <12 mid block, else low), add press height next to PPDA.

## Readiness and fatigue
In professional players the Hooper wellness index (subjective sleep, stress, soreness, fatigue) tracked match fatigue better than resting HRV ([study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6390199/)). Wrist PPG-derived HRV is not fully interchangeable with ECG HRV ([2026 review](https://nhsjs.com/2026/a-low-cost-ppg-based-wearable-framework-for-monitoring-fatigue-and-recovery-in-athletes/)). Heart-rate-only features classified fatigue at about 90% in training ([study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2542660525003610)). No direct evidence found that HRV-guided decisions improve substitution timing.
**Adopted:** a subjective wellness score to readiness next to HRV; pitch HRV as one signal, not proof; live alerts already lean on heart rate and output.

## Open-source match engines
[tbleckert/football-simulator](https://github.com/tbleckert/football-simulator) and [fredGob/foot-match-engine](https://github.com/fredGob/foot-match-engine) expose tactics as numeric instructions (press, width, tempo, defensive line, compactness). [DeltaBitsSystem/FootballEngine](https://github.com/DeltaBitsSystem/FootballEngine) uses target positions plus intents; [abpfiff](https://github.com/davidklemme/abpfiff) composes tactical rules over a spatial control model.
**Adopted:** instruction sliders replace the 3-state "approach" (presets remain) (press, line height, width, tempo) that feed the shapes and positions; our anchor-plus-ball-pull positions match the common pattern. Pitch control is a stretch goal.

## Game plans, roles and box occupation
Styles differ mainly in build-up speed and where the block sits. Counter-attacking: low block, compact, then one or two passes to the final third once the ball is won; it struggles against deep blocks and works best when the opponent has the ball ([Coaches' Voice on the low block](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/low-block-football-tactics-explained-simeone-dyche-mourinho/), [counter-attack analysis](https://thebenchviewsoccer.com/tactics/counter-attack)). Gegenpressing is defined by the seconds after losing the ball and is physically demanding; possession play is slow and patient; direct play uses long balls and early crosses ([style overview](https://artoffootballblog.com/different-styles-of-play-in-soccer/)).
In a 4-4-2 the wide midfielders either attack the space behind the back line to cross, or cut inside and combine; full-backs overlap when the winger goes inside, or underlap. Overlaps should be selective, not both flanks at once ([Premier League explainer](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4243787), [Coaches' Voice 4-4-2](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/4-4-2-football-tactics-ferguson-simeone-hasenhuttl-dyche/)). A back three or five uses wing-backs for the whole flank.
Cutbacks suit deep blocks that pack the area; half-spaces give better angles ([cut-backs](https://the-footballanalyst.com/cut-backs-football-tactics-explained/), [half-spaces](https://www.socceredu.com/en-US/blog/half-space-soccer)). Box occupation heuristic: near post, penalty spot, far post, edge. This is a coaching template, not measured data; no source gave numbers for players in the box per delivery type.
Phases of play (build-up, progression, final third, transitions) are summarised at [The Football Analyst](https://the-footballanalyst.com/the-phases-of-play-football-tactics-explained/).

**Adopted:** game-plan presets (counter, possession, gegenpress, wings, inside) over three new sliders (build-up speed, crosses vs inside, box occupation); full-back overlap / underlap, wide-midfielder byline runs or tucking in, wing-backs, and box targets drawn live on the pitch; player archetypes and fit (`profiles.py`); a counter-attacking style label.
**Not found:** search for real numbers on crosses vs cutbacks, and on rest defence and specific roles, returned nothing usable. The weights in the engine are assumptions.
